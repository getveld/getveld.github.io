A practical decision method

How Veld decides what to change.

Before recommending implementation or product selection, Veld looks for a defensible answer about the work, the cause, the alternatives, the risk, and the evidence that would justify continuing or stopping.

Five questions come first

  1. Which recurring work is materially worth improving?
  2. What is causing the friction: process, ownership, information, system design, or something else?
  3. Which response fits best: simplify, clarify ownership, use an existing feature, automate conventionally, test AI with human review, measure first, or make no change?
  4. What would make a bounded test safe, useful, and measurable?
  5. What evidence would justify continuing, revising, or stopping?

The AI Workflow Opportunity Assessment is designed to produce those decisions before implementation.

The method

Six steps, each with an inspectable output.

Qualify the decision

Establish the business consequence, authority, workflow ownership, urgency, funding readiness, data sensitivity, and whether an assessment is proportionate.

Map the current work

Document triggers, steps, handoffs, systems, waits, rework, exceptions, controls, and desired finishes across the priority workflows.

Establish the evidence

Separate observed facts, system data, samples, estimates, inferences, disputed points, and unknowns. Identify usable baseline candidates.

Compare intervention paths

Compare leaving the workflow unchanged, simplifying it, clarifying ownership, using the current stack, applying deterministic automation, testing AI with human review, or measuring first.

Screen and rank

Evaluate business relevance, frequency, evidence strength, intervention fit, feasibility, adoption, data and security, time to learning, reversibility, and economics.

Define a 30-day decision plan

Name the owner, baseline, test, target, guardrail, review date, stop condition, rollback, and next decision.

The stop screen comes before the score

Some conditions are redesign or stop signals even when projected upside appears attractive:

  • removing required human accountability;
  • unattended high-consequence employment, payment, legal, safety, or customer decisions;
  • prohibited or unjustified data use;
  • actions that cannot be audited, contained, or reversed;
  • an unstable workflow with no accountable owner;
  • no usable baseline or test capacity; or
  • automation that would weaken essential relationship work.

A numeric score does not override these conditions.

What makes a recommendation ready

A high-level idea is not enough. A recommendation needs a supported workflow problem and cause, alternatives considered, accountable owners, evidence and limitations, realistic implementation and recurring costs, data and permission boundaries, human-review controls, measurable targets, stop conditions, containment, and rollback.

If the owner, mechanism, evidence, or data boundary is unknown, the honest recommendation is usually investigate, measure first, or not yet.

How value is represented

Veld separates active labor potentially released, elapsed or queue-time change, direct cost change, quality and rework change, adoption and review overhead, customer or revenue hypotheses, and realized value after implementation.

Recovered time is not automatically cash. Any modeled range must state its source, assumptions, confidence, cost, and validation plan.

What this page proves—and what it does not.

This page shows the decision process Veld is prepared to apply. It does not prove that a particular company has a workflow problem, that AI is appropriate, or that any savings, revenue, quality, security, compliance, or other outcome will result.
